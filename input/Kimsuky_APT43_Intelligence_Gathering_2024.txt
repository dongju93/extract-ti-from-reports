Kimsuky (APT43): Intelligence Gathering Campaign Targeting Korean Peninsula Think Tanks and Policy Research Institutes
Published: 2024-01-10
Source: KISA (Korea Internet & Security Agency) / NCSC Joint Advisory
TLP: WHITE

==============================================================================
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (요약)
==============================================================================

The Korea Internet & Security Agency (KISA) and the National Cyber Security Centre
(NCSC) jointly issue this advisory to warn organizations about an ongoing cyber
espionage campaign attributed to Kimsuky (also known as APT43, Velvet Chollima,
Black Banshee, and Thallium). The campaign specifically targets:

  - Korea-focused policy research institutes and think tanks
  - Academic institutions with Korean peninsula studies programs
  - Government contractors providing policy analysis services
  - Journalists covering North Korean affairs
  - NGOs supporting North Korean defectors

The threat actor employs convincing email impersonation of legitimate Korean
government officials, academic researchers, and journalists to establish initial
contact before delivering malware-laden documents. Observed malware families
include updated variants of AppleSeed, AlphaSeed, and a previously undocumented
browser credential stealer dubbed GOLDDRAGON.

==============================================================================
BACKGROUND: KIMSUKY
==============================================================================

Kimsuky has been active since at least 2012 and is assessed by the US, UK, and
ROK intelligence communities to operate on behalf of the North Korean Reconnaissance
General Bureau (RGB). Unlike Lazarus Group (which focuses on financial theft),
Kimsuky primarily conducts intelligence collection targeting policy, diplomatic,
and military information relevant to North Korean strategic interests.

Key characteristics:
  - Excellent Korean language capability and cultural understanding
  - Impersonation of real, named individuals from target sectors
  - Long-term, patient engagement before malware delivery
  - Use of BabyShark, AppleSeed, FlowerPower, GoldDragon malware families
  - Heavy reliance on free webmail services (Gmail, Naver, Daum) for C2

==============================================================================
TECHNICAL ANALYSIS
==============================================================================

Initial Contact Phase:
Attackers send benign initial emails (no malware) to establish trust. These may
involve requests for document review, interview invitations, or joint research
collaboration. After 1-3 legitimate exchanges, the attacker sends a malicious
document.

Malicious Document:
Spear phishing emails deliver HWP (Hangul Word Processor) files or password-
protected Office documents. HWP files exploit Hangul Office vulnerabilities or
contain embedded OLE objects with malicious scripts.

Observed lure documents in this campaign:
  - "2024년_대북정책_연구보고서_초안.hwp" (Draft NK Policy Research Report 2024)

==============================================================================
GOLDDRAGON TECHNICAL BEHAVIOR
==============================================================================

Upon loading, GOLDDRAGON performs the following:
  1. Copies browser profile directories to %TEMP%\~dumpXXXX\ (randomized folder)
  2. For SQLite-based stores (Chrome, Edge): decrypts using DPAPI and CryptUnprotectData
  3. Compresses collected data using zlib
  4. Exfiltrates via HTTP POST to C2 server with custom headers:
     X-Request-ID: <base64-encoded machine fingerprint>
     X-Session-Token: <base64-encoded encrypted data>
  5. Deletes staging directory and clears event logs (Security, System, Application)

Persistence is achieved via:
  - Scheduled task: "Windows Defender SmartScreen Update" running every 4 hours
  - Registry: HKCU\Software\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Run
